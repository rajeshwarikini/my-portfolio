NOTE / 01 CONVERSATIONAL COMMERCE

Why the best commerce journeys feel less like funnels

Messaging changes more than the interface. It changes how customers explore, decide, and build confidence in a purchase.

Read note ↘

Traditional funnels assume a predictable path: discover, compare, decide, convert. Conversations are different. Customers move backwards, ask for reassurance, change context, and expect the experience to remember what matters.

Strong conversational commerce starts with intent, not automation. The product should make the next decision easier—through relevant choices, clear recovery paths, and a seamless handoff when human judgment adds value.

The most useful metrics follow that behaviour: time to confident action, assisted conversion, drop-off by intent, and whether customers return to the channel.

NOTE / 02 GENERATIVE AI

Start with the workflow, not the model

A useful GenAI roadmap is built around decisions and friction—not a list of impressive technical capabilities.

Read note ↘

The fastest way to lose clarity in an AI initiative is to begin with what a model can do. Begin instead with the work: where people wait, repeat, search, reconcile, or make decisions with incomplete information.

Then frame the opportunity across four dimensions: value to the user, quality required for trust, feasibility at scale, and the cost of getting an answer wrong. This turns a broad AI ambition into a product bet that can be tested.

A strong first release is narrow enough to evaluate honestly, instrumented from day one, and designed to keep people in control.

NOTE / 03 INTERNAL PRODUCTS

Operational efficiency is a product outcome

Internal tools deserve the same discovery, usability, and outcome discipline as customer-facing products.

Read note ↘

Internal products often inherit fragmented processes and hidden workarounds. Automating that complexity without understanding it only makes the wrong workflow move faster.

Map the real journey first—including spreadsheets, messages, approvals, and exception handling. The biggest gains usually come from removing handoffs, improving visibility, and helping people act on the right information.

Measure success in time returned to the team, fewer errors, faster throughput, and greater confidence—not merely feature adoption.
